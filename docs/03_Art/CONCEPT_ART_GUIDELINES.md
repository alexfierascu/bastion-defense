# Concept Art Guidelines

Status: Approved

Version: 1.0

Owner: Art Direction

---

# Purpose

This document defines how concept art is created for Bastion Defense.

Concept art is not marketing artwork.

Concept art exists to solve design problems.

Every illustration should answer questions before production begins.

---

# Philosophy

Concept art should communicate:

- Scale
- Mood
- Architecture
- Materials
- Composition
- Gameplay readability

It should never exist simply because it looks beautiful.

Beauty follows function.

---

# Primary Goals

Every concept should answer at least one of the following:

How does it work?

How does it feel?

How is it built?

How does it age?

How does it support gameplay?

If it answers none of these, it should not exist.

---

# Composition

Every concept has one hero subject.

Everything else supports it.

Never create visual clutter.

Large readable forms come first.

Details come last.

---

# Perspective

Always use believable perspective.

Human eye level whenever possible.

Avoid exaggerated wide-angle lenses.

Avoid distorted proportions.

Architecture must feel buildable.

---

# Materials

Every material should already communicate its weight.

Stone feels heavy.

Wood feels structural.

Iron feels forged.

Leather feels worn.

Canvas feels practical.

Do not rely on labels.

---

# Lighting

Use lighting to explain form.

Never hide important information in shadow.

Warm light represents civilization.

Cold light represents nature.

Lighting should reinforce composition.

---

# Color

Color supports readability.

Never use saturated colors to create interest.

Contrast should come from:

Light.

Scale.

Composition.

Materials.

---

# Iteration Process

Step 1

Thumbnail sketches.

Focus only on composition.

Step 2

Value study.

Ignore color.

Step 3

Material pass.

Step 4

Lighting pass.

Step 5

Final polish.

Never jump directly to the final illustration.

---

# Camera

Concept art should use realistic camera positions.

Avoid impossible viewpoints.

The viewer should feel physically present.

---

# Gameplay Integration

Concept art must support gameplay.

Enemy routes remain readable.

Tower positions remain logical.

Landmarks remain visible.

The player should immediately understand the environment.

---

# Storytelling

Every concept should imply history.

Examples

Recently repaired wall.

Old watchtower.

Weathered bridge.

Moss-covered staircase.

Abandoned siege engine.

Visible maintenance.

Nothing appears freshly built.
