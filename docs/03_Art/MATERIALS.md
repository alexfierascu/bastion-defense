# Materials

Status: Approved

Version: 1.0

Owner: Art Direction

---

# Purpose

This document defines every material used in Bastion Defense.

Materials communicate:

- Age
- Quality
- Function
- Civilization
- Maintenance
- History

Players should immediately understand what an object is made from.

Materials should never feel generic.

---

# Philosophy

Materials are honest.

Stone looks heavy.

Wood looks structural.

Iron looks forged.

Bronze looks valuable.

Leather looks worn.

Canvas looks practical.

Every material should immediately communicate its purpose.

---

# Material Hierarchy

Primary Materials

- Granite
- Limestone
- Oak
- Iron
- Bronze

Secondary Materials

- Leather
- Canvas
- Rope
- Clay
- Slate
- Glass

Natural Materials

- Moss
- Roots
- Bark
- Grass
- Soil
- Water

Never introduce materials that do not belong in the world.

---

# Stone

Role

Primary construction material.

Used For

Walls.

Towers.

Foundations.

Bridges.

Retaining walls.

Visual Characteristics

Heavy.

Cold.

Weathered.

Chipped edges.

Visible repairs.

Rounded corners after centuries.

Never perfectly flat.

---

# Limestone

Used For

Decorative trims.

Windows.

Stairs.

Statues.

Visual Characteristics

Lighter than granite.

More porous.

Ages faster.

Collects moss easily.

---

# Oak

Role

Primary structural wood.

Used For

Doors.

Beams.

Bridges.

Roofs.

Watch towers.

Visual Characteristics

Deep grain.

Visible knots.

Darkens with age.

Never polished.

---

# Iron

Role

Structural reinforcement.

Used For

Brackets.

Hinges.

Chains.

Gate reinforcements.

Armor.

Weapons.

Visual Characteristics

Dark.

Forged.

Hammer marks.

Surface rust.

Never chrome.

---

# Bronze

Role

Premium metal.

Used For

Important hardware.

Decorative reinforcement.

Commander equipment.

Gate details.

Visual Characteristics

Warm.

Oxidized.

Subtle green patina.

Used sparingly.

---

# Leather

Used For

Belts.

Scabbards.

Armor.

Bags.

Horse equipment.

Visual Characteristics

Cracked.

Soft edges.

Color variation.

Visible stitching.

---

# Canvas

Used For

Banners.

Awnings.

Military tents.

Training equipment.

Visual Characteristics

Wrinkled.

Sun faded.

Water stained.

Never perfectly clean.

---

# Rope

Used For

Bridges.

Winches.

Cranes.

Lifts.

Supplies.

Visual Characteristics

Twisted fibers.

Frayed ends.

Natural sag.

---

# Slate

Used For

Roofs.

Drainage.

Walkways.

Visual Characteristics

Dark gray.

Layered.

Uneven.

Moisture darkens the surface.

---

# Glass

Glass is rare.

Used only where practical.

Examples

Signal lanterns.

Windows.

Greenhouses.

Never large decorative stained glass.

---

# Moss

Represents time.

Appears on

Stone.

Roofs.

North-facing walls.

Bridge supports.

Never uniformly.

---

# Roots

Represent cooperation.

Appear

Under bridges.

Along walls.

Near foundations.

Around ancient structures.

Roots support.

They never destroy.

---

# Soil

Soil reflects usage.

Roads

Compacted.

Gardens

Rich.

Forest

Organic.

Construction sites

Loose.

Rain changes appearance.

---

# Water

Water reflects weather.

Calm water

Mirror reflections.

Wind

Small ripples.

Rain

Surface disturbance.

Water should never appear static.

---

# Weathering Rules

Every material changes over time.

Stone

Chips.

Wood

Cracks.

Iron

Rusts.

Bronze

Oxidizes.

Leather

Softens.

Canvas

Fades.

Nothing remains untouched.

# Material Pairing

Preferred combinations

Stone + Moss

Stone + Roots

Oak + Iron

Bronze + Oak

Canvas + Rope

Leather + Iron

Avoid

Stone + Bright Gold

Chrome + Wood

Marble + Military Structures

Bright Painted Surfaces

Plastic-like finishes

---

# Gameplay Readability

Interactive materials should remain recognizable.

Wood

Destructible.

Stone

Permanent.

Metal

Reinforced.

Canvas

Flexible.

Water

Traversal boundary.

Material language should improve gameplay clarity.

---

# Performance

Reuse material libraries.

Limit unique shaders.

Prefer tileable textures.

Use decals for variation.

Avoid unique textures for every object.

---

# AI Rules

Every generated asset must answer:

Is the material believable?

Has it aged naturally?

Would it exist in this climate?

Does it support the Art Bible?

Would a medieval builder choose this material?

If any answer is "No", redesign the asset.
