# Difficulty Philosophy

Status: Approved

Version: 1.0

Owner: Game Design

---

# Purpose

This document defines how difficulty is designed in Bastion Defense.

Difficulty should emerge from meaningful decisions.

Not from unfair mechanics.

Not from inflated numbers.

---

# Philosophy

A difficult game is one that asks difficult questions.

Not one that gives impossible answers.

Players should lose because of their decisions.

Never because the game cheated.

---

# Design Goals

Difficulty should reward:

Planning.

Observation.

Adaptation.

Resource management.

Positioning.

Knowledge.

Execution.

Every victory should feel earned.

Every defeat should teach something.

---

# Sources of Difficulty

Acceptable

- Enemy composition
- Enemy synergy
- Multiple objectives
- Resource scarcity
- Terrain
- Limited build space
- Time pressure
- Boss mechanics
- Environmental hazards

Unacceptable

- Hidden information
- Random unavoidable damage
- Instant failure
- Invisible mechanics
- Unreadable attacks
- Artificial input delay
- Health inflation without purpose

---

# Skill Curve

Beginner

Learn mechanics.

Intermediate

Learn efficiency.

Advanced

Learn optimization.

Expert

Learn adaptation.

Master

Learn prediction.

Difficulty increases through knowledge.

Not reaction speed.

---

# Player Knowledge

Players should always know:

What happened.

Why it happened.

How to improve.

What to try next.

Failure without understanding is bad design.

---

# Enemy Scaling

Enemies become harder by:

Better abilities.

New synergies.

Improved formations.

Mixed compositions.

Unique behaviors.

Avoid simple HP inflation.

Avoid simple damage inflation.

---

# Economy Pressure

Resources become valuable.

Every purchase has opportunity cost.

Players should constantly evaluate:

Build now?

Save?

Upgrade?

Repair?

Recruit?

There should rarely be one obvious answer.

---

# Map Difficulty

Maps become harder through:

Terrain.

Multiple lanes.

Longer routes.

Split routes.

Verticality.

Weather.

Limited resources.

Never through unreadable layouts.

---

# Boss Design

Bosses test understanding.

Not patience.

Bosses should have:

Readable attacks.

Clear weaknesses.

Counterplay.

Preparation opportunities.

Multiple strategies.

---

# RNG Philosophy

Randomness creates variety.

Never outcomes.

Examples

Good

Enemy wave order changes.

Loot variation.

Weather variation.

Bad

Critical random failures.

Random tower accuracy.

Unavoidable random deaths.

---

# Recovery

Players should have opportunities to recover.

One mistake should not immediately lose the match.

Poor long-term planning should.

---

# Accessibility

Difficulty should challenge strategy.

Not physical ability.

Support:

Pause.

Speed controls.

Colorblind modes.

Reduced motion.

Scalable UI.

Difficulty options.

---

# Difficulty Levels

Story

Relaxed.

Forgiving.

Focus on learning.

Explorer

Standard experience.

Recommended for first playthrough.

Veteran

Requires efficient planning.

Smaller margin for error.

Commander

Designed for experienced players.

Enemies fully utilize mechanics.

Legend

Designed for mastery.

Assumes complete understanding of all systems.

No artificial cheats.

Only better challenges.

---

# AI Rules

When designing new enemies or mechanics ask:

Does this create an interesting decision?

Can the player understand it?

Can the player prepare for it?

Does it have counterplay?

Would losing feel fair?

If any answer is "No", redesign.

When players lose, their immediate reaction should be:

"I know exactly what I did wrong."

Not:

"That was unfair."
