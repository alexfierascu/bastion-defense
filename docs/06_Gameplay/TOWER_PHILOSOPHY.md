# Tower Philosophy

Status: Approved

Version: 1.0

Owner: Game Design

---

# Purpose

This document defines the design philosophy behind every defensive structure in Bastion Defense.

Towers are not weapons.

Towers are specialized tools that solve tactical problems.

Every tower must justify its existence.

---

# Philosophy

No tower is universally best.

Every tower has:

- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Counters
- Synergies
- Ideal positioning

If a tower becomes the correct answer in every situation,

it is overpowered.

---

# Design Goals

Every tower should:

Create interesting decisions.

Encourage experimentation.

Reward planning.

Support team compositions.

Remain readable.

Stay useful throughout the game.

---

# Tower Roles

Every tower belongs to one primary role.

## Damage

Removes enemies.

Examples

Arrow Tower

Ballista

Cannon

---

## Crowd Control

Manipulates enemies.

Examples

Slow Tower

Frost Tower

Net Launcher

---

## Area Denial

Controls terrain.

Examples

Spike Trap

Oil Thrower

Fire Pit

---

## Support

Improves allies.

Examples

Banner Tower

Watch Bell

Supply Depot

Healing Shrine

---

## Economy

Generates long-term value.

Examples

Trade Post

Lumber Mill

Stone Quarry

---

## Detection

Reveals hidden enemies.

Tracks movement.

Provides vision.

---

# Design Formula

Every tower should answer:

What problem does it solve?

When is it strongest?

When is it weakest?

What towers complement it?

What enemies counter it?

---

# Placement

Tower placement is gameplay.

Not decoration.

Good placement should outperform expensive upgrades.

Poor placement should always have consequences.

---

# Upgrades

Upgrades should change behavior.

Not simply increase numbers.

Good upgrades unlock:

New targeting.

New mechanics.

New synergies.

New strategies.

Avoid:

+10 Damage

+20% Attack Speed

+15 Range

Unless accompanied by gameplay changes.

---

# Synergy

The strongest defenses come from combinations.

Examples

Slow

↓

Splash Damage

Armor Break

↓

Ballista

Poison

↓

Long Routes

Barracks

↓

Support Tower

Every tower should have at least three meaningful synergies.

---

# Counterplay

Every tower should struggle against something.

Examples

Arrow Tower

Weak against armored units.

Cannon

Weak against fast enemies.

Barracks

Weak against flying enemies.

Support Tower

Weak alone.

Weaknesses create strategy.

---

# Readability

Players should identify a tower by:

Silhouette.

Animation.

Projectile.

Sound.

Color accents.

Not by reading text.

---

# Economy

Every tower has:

Construction Cost.

Upgrade Cost.

Maintenance (optional).

Opportunity Cost.

Expensive towers should solve expensive problems.

---

# Visual Identity

Every tower should visually communicate:

Role.

Technology level.

Power.

Upgrade level.

Faction identity.

A player should understand a tower before selecting it.

---

# AI Rules

Before creating a new tower ask:

Does another tower already solve this problem?

Does this create a new strategy?

Does it encourage interesting decisions?

Does it have meaningful weaknesses?

Would experienced players use it?

If any answer is "No",

redesign it.

"Which tower is the strongest?"

"Which tower is strongest here?"
