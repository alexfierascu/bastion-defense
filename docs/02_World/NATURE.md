# Nature

Status: Approved

Version: 1.0

Owner: Creative Direction

---

# Purpose

This document defines the relationship between nature and civilization in Bastion Defense.

Nature is not a biome.

Nature is not decoration.

Nature is one of the primary characters of the game.

Every tree, root, flower, river and stone exists for a reason.

---

# Core Philosophy

Nature chose to protect humanity.

The Bastion was not built against the forest.

It was built with the forest.

Stone and roots coexist.

Walls and moss coexist.

Civilizations survive because they learned to respect nature instead of conquering it.

---

# Design Principles

Nature should always feel:

Alive

Ancient

Protective

Patient

Believable

Balanced

Never:

Hostile by default

Chaotic

Overgrown

Jungle-like

Fantasy exaggerated

Magical for spectacle

---

# Living Ecosystem

Everything belongs to an ecosystem.

Trees influence light.

Light influences flowers.

Flowers attract insects.

Insects attract birds.

Birds spread seeds.

Rain feeds rivers.

Rivers feed forests.

Nothing exists in isolation.

---

# The Great Forest

The Bastion is surrounded by an ancient forest.

The forest is older than the fortress.

Some trees existed before civilization.

The oldest trees are considered sacred.

Logging is carefully controlled.

Every tree removed is replaced.

---

# Trees

Trees have identity.

Oak

The protector.

Large canopy.

Deep roots.

Used in construction.

Ash

Flexible.

Fast growing.

Used for tools and bows.

Birch

Bright bark.

Common near rivers.

Pine

Mountain regions.

Wind resistant.

Evergreen.

Willow

Near lakes.

Streams.

Marshes.

No fantasy tree species.

---

# Roots

Roots are symbolic.

They connect the Bastion with nature.

Roots reinforce:

Walls

Bridges

Watch towers

Retaining walls

Cliffs

They never destroy architecture.

They strengthen it.

---

# Grass

Grass follows terrain.

Short in frequently used areas.

Tall in abandoned places.

Different species appear depending on moisture and sunlight.

Wind affects grass in waves.

Never uniformly.

---

# Flowers

Flowers communicate peace.

Battlefields contain fewer flowers.

Gardens contain more.

White flowers

Near shrines.

Yellow flowers

Open fields.

Blue flowers

Near streams.

Red flowers

Rare.

Never decorative for the sake of color.

# Environmental Storytelling

Nature records history.

Examples

A tree growing through abandoned stone.

Roots covering an old watchtower.

Wildflowers on forgotten graves.

A bridge repaired around an ancient oak.

Moss covering an unused staircase.

Players should discover stories without dialogue.

---

# Relationship with Architecture

Nature never consumes the Bastion.

Nature supports it.

Examples

Roots reinforce retaining walls.

Trees provide shade.

Bushes stabilize slopes.

Flowers mark memorials.

Gardens improve morale.

Architecture and nature cooperate.

---

# Relationship with Gameplay

Nature should improve readability.

Trees frame important locations.

Grass indicates walkable terrain.

Flowers draw subtle attention.

Rivers define enemy routes.

Cliffs explain map boundaries.

Gameplay never contradicts the environment.

---

# Audio

Nature provides constant ambience.

Wind.

Leaves.

Birds.

Water.

Insects.

Tree creaks.

Rain.

Distant thunder.

Silence remains important.

Avoid continuous loud ambience.

---

# AI Rules

When generating natural assets, ask:

Could this exist naturally?

Does it support the world?

Does it help the player understand the environment?

Does it improve composition?

Does it respect the ecosystem?

If any answer is "No", reject the asset.

Never generate random decorative vegetation.

Every plant belongs somewhere.

A player should believe the forest would continue living even if the player never existed.

Nature should feel independent.

Ancient.

Protective.

Alive.

The player is defending civilization inside a living ecosystem, not inside a castle surrounded by decorative trees.
