# History

Status: Approved

Version: 1.0

Owner: Creative Direction

---

# Purpose

This document defines the historical foundation of Bastion Defense.

History exists to explain the present world.

It should never overwhelm the player with unnecessary lore.

Every historical event must explain something visible in the game.

If the player cannot see its consequences, it should not exist.

---

# Core Philosophy

History is discovered.

Not narrated.

Players should learn about the world by observing:

- Damaged walls
- Ancient roads
- Forgotten towers
- Old battlefields
- Statues
- Bridges
- Memorials
- Architecture
- Veteran NPC dialogue

The world should feel older than the player.

---

# The Ancient Wilds

Long before civilizations existed, the land belonged entirely to nature.

Great forests covered the continent.

Massive rivers carved valleys.

Wildlife flourished.

The oldest trees still standing today were already ancient before the first human settlement.

Nature was balanced.

There were no kingdoms.

No roads.

No walls.

---

# The First Settlers

Small communities slowly appeared along rivers.

People lived from:

- Hunting
- Farming
- Fishing
- Forestry

Villages were temporary.

Communities were isolated.

Nature was respected because survival depended on it.

---

# The Great Expansion

Generations later, villages became towns.

Roads connected settlements.

Trade increased.

Stone replaced wood.

Knowledge spread.

Architecture improved.

Civilization expanded.

People believed the wilderness could be conquered.

This belief proved false.

---

# The Years of Conflict

As civilization expanded, conflict became inevitable.

Resources became scarce.

Trade routes required protection.

Fortifications appeared.

Watchtowers overlooked roads.

Walls surrounded towns.

Kingdoms rose.

Kingdoms fell.

Most disappeared completely.

Very little remains today.

---

# The Founding of the Bastion

Military engineers searched for a location capable of surviving a prolonged siege.

They selected a valley naturally protected by:

- Mountains
- Dense forests
- A river
- High ground

Construction began around an enormous ancient oak.

Instead of cutting it down, they built around it.

Over generations, the Great Tree became the symbol of coexistence between humanity and nature.

The fortress became known simply as:

The Bastion.

---

# The Living Alliance

During construction, something unexpected happened.

Tree roots slowly intertwined with foundations.

Stone remained stable.

Walls cracked less.

Bridges settled naturally.

The surrounding forest appeared healthier.

Whether this was natural or something deeper remains unknown.

No one attempted to separate the two again.

From that moment onward, every expansion respected the forest.

---

# The First Siege

The Bastion was tested sooner than expected.

A massive invading army surrounded the fortress.

The siege lasted months.

The defenders never surrendered.

Food reserves held.

Water remained available.

The walls endured.

The attackers eventually withdrew.

This victory established the Bastion as the safest place in the known world.

---

# The Long Peace

Centuries followed with relatively few large wars.

Trade flourished.

Engineers improved defenses.

Blacksmiths refined equipment.

Carpenters rebuilt older structures.

Nature reclaimed abandoned battlefields.

Villages prospered under the Bastion's protection.

Many of today's roads originate from this era.

---

# The Ashen Wars

Peace eventually ended.

A coalition of warbands emerged from the frontier.

These tribes rejected permanent settlements.

They believed civilization weakened humanity.

Together they formed what would later become known as:

The Ashen Horde.

The Bastion endured repeated assaults.

Each attack left visible scars.

Every repaired wall seen today originates from this period.

---

# The Era of Repairs

Rather than rebuilding damaged sections completely, engineers repaired them.

Different generations used different techniques.

As a result, the Bastion became a visible timeline.

Older stone.

Newer stone.

Different mortar.

Replacement beams.

Iron reinforcement.

Nothing was hidden.

Repairs became part of the fortress's identity.

---

# Present Day

The player begins during a period of uneasy stability.

The Bastion still stands.

Villages remain active.

Trade continues.

But attacks are becoming more frequent.

Scouts report larger enemy movements.

Supply routes are under pressure.

The world is changing.

The player becomes part of this new chapter.

---

# Historical Design Rules

History should always explain visible details.

Examples:

Why is this bridge wider?

Because supply wagons increased.

Why is this tower newer?

Because the original collapsed.

Why are these stones different?

Because they were repaired after a siege.

Why does moss cover this wall?

Because it has remained untouched for decades.

Every visual element should have a historical reason.

---

# Things History Should Never Include

Ancient gods controlling kingdoms.

World-ending prophecies.

Chosen heroes.

Secret bloodlines.

Hundreds of forgotten empires.

Magic wars.

Flying cities.

Reality-ending events.

History should remain grounded.

---

# Relationship With Gameplay

Progression represents another chapter in the Bastion's history.

As players advance:

Walls improve.

Roads expand.

Workshops evolve.

Defenses become more sophisticated.

The player contributes to history instead of simply reading about it.

---

# Relationship With Architecture

Architecture is the physical record of history.

Damage is preserved.

Repairs remain visible.

Old foundations remain beneath newer structures.

Buildings expand naturally over time.

Nothing should appear recently constructed unless gameplay explains it.

---

# Relationship With Nature

Nature remembers.

Trees grow around abandoned structures.

Roots reinforce repaired walls.

Flowers bloom over forgotten battlefields.

The landscape quietly records the passing centuries.

---

# AI Rules

When generating historical assets, ask:

What happened here?

Why was it repaired?

Who built it?

How old is it?

What evidence remains today?

If these questions cannot be answered, redesign the asset.

# Success Criteria

A player should understand the world's history without reading a single line of dialogue.

History should exist in:

Architecture.

Nature.

Roads.

Damage.

Repairs.

Landmarks.

The world itself should be the history book.
