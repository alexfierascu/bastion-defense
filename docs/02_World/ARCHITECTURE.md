# Architecture

Status: Approved

Version: 1.0

Owner: Creative Direction

---

# Purpose

This document defines every architectural principle used throughout Bastion Defense.

It exists to ensure that every building, tower, wall, bridge, gate and structure feels as though it belongs to the same civilization.

Architecture is never decoration.

Architecture always exists to solve a problem.

---

# Core Philosophy

The player should believe:

"This fortress could exist."

Every structure has a purpose.

Every support carries weight.

Every beam supports a roof.

Every staircase reaches a destination.

Every tower watches something.

Every window exists for light, defense or ventilation.

If a structure cannot explain its existence, it should not exist.

---

# Design Pillars

## Function before Beauty

Beauty emerges from engineering.

Never the opposite.

---

## Military First

The Bastion is a fortress.

Defense influences every architectural decision.

Walls are thick.

Corners are protected.

Sightlines are intentional.

Movement is controlled.

---

## Organic Growth

The Bastion has expanded for centuries.

It was never built in one phase.

Every generation improved it.

Architecture should reflect this evolution.

Older stone blends into newer repairs.

Roads widen naturally.

Buildings expand where needed.

Nothing should appear master-planned.

---

## Human Scale

Everything must feel usable.

Doors can be opened.

Stairs can be climbed.

Windows can be reached.

Furniture can be used.

Avoid exaggerated fantasy proportions.

---

# Structural Language

Every structure follows this hierarchy.

Foundation

↓

Walls

↓

Supports

↓

Floors

↓

Roof

↓

Defensive Elements

↓

Decorative Elements

Decoration is always last.

---

# Foundations

Every building sits on visible foundations.

No floating structures.

Foundations follow terrain.

Rock is preferred whenever possible.

Water drainage is always considered.

---

# Walls

Walls communicate strength.

Minimum visual thickness:

Outer Wall

Extremely thick.

Inner Wall

Heavy.

Civilian Building

Medium.

Storage

Heavy.

Watch Tower

Heavy.

Walls never appear paper thin.

---

# Corners

Corners are reinforced.

Large stones.

Additional buttressing.

Corner towers where appropriate.

Corners are natural weak points.

Architecture acknowledges this.

---

# Towers

Every tower has a purpose.

Observation

Signal fires

Archery

Command

Storage

Bell tower

Never build identical towers.

Each should communicate its role through silhouette.

---

# Gatehouses

The strongest building in the fortress.

Contains:

Dual gates

Portcullis

Arrow slits

Guard rooms

Murder holes

Storage

Signal bells

Emergency supplies

Failure at the gate should never mean immediate defeat.

---

# Bridges

Bridges solve terrain problems.

They never exist as decoration.

Stone bridges:

Permanent.

Major routes.

Wood bridges:

Secondary routes.

Repairable.

Temporary military bridges:

Campaign only.

---

# Roofs

Materials

Slate

Oak shingles

Wood planks

Never shiny.

Never colorful.

Snow accumulates naturally.

Rain darkens materials.

---

# Roads

Roads emerge from usage.

Major roads

Stone.

Secondary roads

Packed earth.

Forest trails

Natural soil.

Road width communicates importance.

---

# Stairs

Stairs follow realistic dimensions.

They fit human movement.

Never absurdly steep.

Landings appear on long climbs.

Handrails where appropriate.

---

# Windows

Windows exist for:

Light

Ventilation

Observation

Defense

Nothing else.

Large decorative glass windows are forbidden.

---

# Doors

Heavy oak.

Iron bands.

Visible hinges.

Weathered surfaces.

Frequent use creates polished handles.

Unused doors collect moss.

# Material Aging

Stone

Darkens with moisture.

Wood

Cracks.

Iron

Rusts slowly.

Bronze

Develops patina.

Slate

Collects moss.

No material remains pristine forever.

---

# Weather Effects

Rain

Darkens stone.

Snow

Accumulates on horizontal surfaces.

Wind

Moves banners.

Sun

Fades exposed wood.

Fog

Softens distant silhouettes.

Architecture should react to weather.

---

# Damage Language

Damage tells stories.

Types

Arrow impacts

Burn marks

Broken crenellations

Collapsed roofs

Cracked stone

Emergency repairs

Replaced beams

No random destruction.

Every scar has a reason.

---

# Repair Language

Repairs remain visible.

Fresh stone differs from old stone.

Replacement beams never perfectly match.

Metal plates reinforce cracked masonry.

Civilizations repair.

They do not rebuild from scratch.

---

# Interior Principles

Interiors prioritize utility.

Storage close to entrances.

Sleeping areas away from noise.

Workshops near courtyards.

Command areas elevated.

Light enters naturally.

Smoke exits efficiently.

---

# Exterior Principles

Exterior architecture should create memorable silhouettes.

Recognizable landmarks:

Main Gate

Great Tree

Bell Tower

Command Hall

North Watch

Bridge

Players should navigate using landmarks rather than signs.

---

# Construction Rules

Every new structure added to the game must answer:

Why was it built?

Who uses it?

How is it supplied?

How is it defended?

How is it maintained?

If these questions cannot be answered, redesign it.

---

# Future Buildings

Every future tower or structure must inherit this language.

Examples

Arrow Tower

Ballista Tower

Mage Archive

Barracks

Workshop

Temple

Library

Market

Each should immediately feel like part of the Bastion.

---

# AI Rules

Never generate:

Floating structures.

Thin castle walls.

Impossible supports.

Symmetrical fantasy castles.

Golden ornamental roofs.

Oversized statues.

Massive empty halls.

Instead generate:

Practical engineering.

Visible history.

Weathered materials.

Purpose-driven layouts.

Natural aging.

Believable construction.

# Success Criteria

A player should instantly recognize a Bastion structure by:

Its engineering.

Its proportions.

Its materials.

Its weathering.

Its functionality.

Its relationship with the surrounding landscape.

If a building could be placed into another fantasy game without anyone noticing, it has failed the Bastion architectural identity.
